\section{Motivation and hypothesis}
Practitioners want the \emph{slowly-varying state} of a time series ---
machine health, cardiac diagnosis, activity, regime --- but the observed
signal buries it under fast variation (per-revolution vibration, beat
morphology, sensor noise). Several important tasks need the two \emph{separated},
not merely both encoded: (a) \textbf{anomaly attribution} --- is an alarm a
point anomaly (instantaneous glitch) or a contextual one (slow drift away from
the current state)? the correct response differs; (b) \textbf{selective
robustness} --- a diagnosis that survives a change in the fast dynamics (new
sensor, new subject); (c) interpretation of a health trajectory. An entangled
embedding cannot provide these regardless of its accuracy.

Existing separators fall short in principle. Signal decomposition
(moving-average trend, wavelets, EMD) splits by \emph{frequency band}, but a
slow factor need not live in a low band --- in bearings the degradation state
is the \emph{envelope of high-frequency resonance} (why envelope analysis
exists), so a low-pass ``trend'' misses it. Sequential VAEs (DSAE, C-DSVAE)
separate a \emph{time-invariant} content code via reconstruction ELBOs ---
fragile under sensor noise and unable to represent factors that vary slowly
rather than never. Horizon-conditioned JEPAs (HEPA) internalize multi-scale
dynamics implicitly but do not factor them.

\textbf{Positioning.} Our criterion descends from two classical lines.
Slow feature analysis (Wiskott \& Sejnowski, 2002) extracts \emph{slowly
varying} features, but slowness of the feature trajectory is not the same as
long-range predictive value (our SFA control, Tab.~1, trades the regime away).
The predictive-information / past--future information-bottleneck line (Bialek
et al., 2001; Creutzig et al., 2009) identifies the far-predictive subspace as
the behaviorally relevant one --- our gate is an architectural implementation
of that criterion inside a modern latent-predictive learner, with the horizon
knob as the only supervision. On the identifiability side, nonlinear ICA with
temporal structure (TCL, Hyv\"arinen \& Morioka, 2016; SlowVAE, Klindt et al.,
2021) offers provable recovery under specific generative assumptions, and
Locatello et al.\ (2019) prove unsupervised disentanglement impossible without
some inductive bias; ours is the timescale gap itself. Section~\ref{sec:theory}
gives the two population-level results we can prove --- inclusion of the slow
belief in the ungated block, and the impossibility of exclusion from the
objective alone --- and we are explicit that a full identifiability theorem
(uniqueness of the representation up to symmetry, in the TCL/SlowVAE sense)
remains open; the propositions delimit exactly which half of H1 is structural
and which half is empirical.

We use a purely functional criterion: \textbf{information useful for
predicting the far future is exactly the slow information}, and the prediction
horizon --- free in any predictive learner --- is the supervision. Our
hypothesis:

\emph{H1: in a horizon-conditioned JEPA, if the predictor's access to one
latent block is gated off for long horizons, slow factors concentrate in the
ungated block and fast factors are displaced to the gated block --- no labels.}

Two secondary hypotheses sharpen this. \emph{H2 (assignment asymmetry):} the
gate structurally \emph{assigns} slow information to the ungated block
(inclusion); \emph{exclusion} of fast information is not enforced by the gate
itself --- it relies on the block being a narrow bottleneck and, if needed, a
light decorrelation penalty. \emph{H3
(architecture specificity):} the gate should work on \emph{latent}-space
prediction but fail on raw-signal prediction, because raw targets force the
encoder to smear unpredictable detail across all dimensions.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Backbone (established).} We use a horizon-conditioned JEPA: a causal
encoder produces one embedding per patch; a predictor $P(z_t,\Delta)$ maps an
anchor embedding and a horizon $\Delta$ to a \emph{parallel, direct} prediction
of the target embedding at $t+\Delta$ (not autoregressive next-token). This is
the CPC multi-horizon predictor (van den Oord et al., 2018) with I-JEPA-style
EMA targets (Assran et al., 2023), as in HEPA (2026). \textbf{Our contribution}
is a gate on this backbone, not the backbone.
A causal encoder maps patches to embeddings
$z_t=[z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t;z^{\mathrm{fast}}_t]\in\mathbb R^{64}$ (slow block
deliberately narrow, 16 dims). The predictor, conditioned on
$\Delta\in\{1,4,16,64,128\}$ patches, regresses the EMA target encoder's
embedding at $t+\Delta$:
\[
\mathcal L=\textstyle\sum_{\Delta}\big\|
P\big(z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t,\;g(\Delta)\,z^{\mathrm{fast}}_t,\;\Delta\big)
- \bar z_{t+\Delta}\big\|^2
+\lambda\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dcor}},
\]
with a soft gate $g(\Delta)=\sigma\!\big((\tau-\Delta)/w\big)$: for
$\Delta\!\gg\!\tau$ the predictor sees only the slow block. The gate is
asymmetric by design --- short-horizon prediction keeps access to both blocks
because fast dynamics are \emph{conditioned on} slow state; blocking the slow
block at short horizons would force slow information to be duplicated into
the fast block. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dcor}}$ penalizes squared cross-covariance
between blocks (removes duplicates; cf.\ H2). The threshold is set from the
data itself as $\tau = c\,T_{\mathrm{ac}}$, where $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ is the
signal's autocorrelation decay time (the lifetime of fast dynamics), $c\!\approx\!2.5$;
no sampling-rate knowledge is needed.

\section{Theory: what the gate does and does not guarantee}
\label{sec:theory}
We formalize H2 at the population level. Let $X_{\le t}$ denote the observed
past, and let the generative process have a slow factor $s_t$ (finite state,
as in our pilot; the argument extends to compact state spaces) and fast
factors $f_t$, with:

\emph{(A1) Timescale gap.} There is $\Delta_0$ such that for all
$\Delta\ge\Delta_0$ the far future is conditionally independent of the past
given the current slow state: $Y_\Delta \perp X_{\le t} \mid s_t$, where
$Y_\Delta$ is the (fixed, measurable) prediction target at $t+\Delta$ (raw
future or target-encoder embedding). Equivalently, all far-predictive
information flows through $s_t$; this is what ``fast dynamics mix within
$\Delta_0$'' means, and what $\tau=c\,T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ estimates.

\emph{(A2) Closed gate.} At some trained horizon $\Delta^*\ge\Delta_0$ the
gate is closed, so the predictor input is $z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t=
\phi(X_{\le t})\in\mathbb R^{k}$ alone, and the predictor class is rich
(all measurable functions; our MLP head approximates this).

\textbf{Proposition 1 (inclusion: the gate assigns the slow belief to the
ungated block).} Under A1--A2, with square loss, any population minimizer of
the $\Delta^*$ term satisfies
$\mathbb E[Y_{\Delta^*}\mid z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t]
 =\mathbb E[Y_{\Delta^*}\mid X_{\le t}]$ a.s.
If moreover the regime-conditional target means
$m_i=\mathbb E[Y_{\Delta^*}\mid s_t=i]$ are affinely independent, then the
posterior belief $\pi_t=P(s_t=\cdot\mid X_{\le t})$ is a measurable function
of $z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t$: the ungated block contains \emph{everything the
past says about the slow factor}.
\emph{Proof.} With square loss the optimal predictor is
$P^*(z)=\mathbb E[Y\mid z^{\mathrm{slow}}=z]$, and since
$z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t$ is $X_{\le t}$-measurable, the excess loss decomposes
as $\mathbb E\|\mathbb E[Y\mid z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t]-\mathbb E[Y\mid
X_{\le t}]\|^2\ge0$, with equality iff the two conditional means agree a.s.;
a $k\ge|\mathcal S|-1$ encoder attains it by computing $\pi_t$. By A1,
$\mathbb E[Y\mid X_{\le t}]=\sum_i\pi_t(i)\,m_i$, which is injective in
$\pi_t$ when $\{m_i\}$ are affinely independent. $\square$

\textbf{Proposition 2 (no exclusion: the objective cannot remove fast
information).} Fix any minimizer $(\phi^*,P^*)$. For any measurable fast
statistic $h(X_{\le t})$ there is another minimizer whose slow block also
encodes $h$: take a Borel isomorphism
$\iota:\mathbb R^{k}\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^{k}$ and set
$\phi'=\iota(\phi^*,h)$, $P'=P^*\circ\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\iota^{-1}$ --- identical loss
at every horizon. Hence membership of fast information in
$z^{\mathrm{slow}}$ is \emph{not identified} by the gated objective; no gate
schedule fixes this. The dcor penalty selects, among these optima, ones with
zero cross-covariance between blocks --- it removes \emph{linearly readable}
duplication only, and injections $h$ decorrelated with $z^{\mathrm{fast}}$
survive it. $\square$

Proposition 1 is the mechanism behind F1/F4 (``the gate \emph{assigns}'');
Proposition 2 predicts exactly what we measure: near-complete linear
exclusion under dcor, yet substantial nonlinear recovery (MLP $0.45$, MINE
$0.95$ nats, F4) --- the residual leak is not an implementation failure but
the objective's identified/unidentified boundary. Exclusion beyond the
linear level must come from capacity pressure (narrow $k$; F4's width sweep)
or an explicit nonlinear-dependence penalty, which Proposition~2 motivates
as future work. Corollary: without the gate no horizon ever constrains a
specific block, so the slow belief lands anywhere in $z$ --- the arbitrary
splits seen in the ungated control. Both statements are population-level and
ignore network continuity: a continuous encoder of narrow width cannot
implement the pathological Borel injections of Proposition~2, which is
precisely why the small-$k$ bottleneck excludes in practice (F4) beyond what
the objective identifies.

\section{Pilot setup}
\textbf{Data.} Synthetic series with recorded ground-truth factors: a slow
3-state Markov regime (mean dwell 3000 steps) setting carrier frequency
($\pm5\%$), and a fast OU process $u$ (lifetime 50 steps) that jitters the
instantaneous frequency by a comparable amount and adds a small direct term.
Amplitudes are identical across regimes, so \emph{no short-window statistic
reveals the regime}; identifying it requires integrating hundreds of steps.
Fast ground truth is $(u,\sin\varphi,\cos\varphi)$.
\textbf{Protocol.} Train on 1M steps; evaluate on a held-out 200k-step series.
Linear probes read each block; we report \emph{absolute} scores (regime
accuracy, factor $R^2$). Probe train/test use \textbf{disjoint windows with a
contiguous time split} (real datasets use group splits: HAPT by subject,
PTB-XL by patient, XJTU by bearing) so no near-duplicate window crosses the
split. We monitor RankMe (effective rank) to rule out partial collapse
inflating leak ratios. All variants share one backbone ($\sim$0.5M params),
recipe, and compute; $n{=}3$ seeds.
\textbf{Variants.} Ours (gate$+$dcor, $\lambda{=}4$); gate only; no gate
(= ungated horizon-JEPA, i.e.\ HEPA-style; its ``slow'' block is the same 16
indices, controlling for arbitrary splits); dcor only; and, for H3, an AR head
predicting raw future patches (next-token family) with and without the
identical gate.

\section{Preliminary results}
\textbf{F1 --- the gate separates (H1).} With gate$+$dcor, $z_{\mathrm{slow}}$
(16 of 64 dims) keeps the slow factor (regime acc $0.80$, matching the full
embedding's $0.69$) while fast-factor leakage falls to $R^2\,0.19$ ($u$) and
$0.27$ (phase), vs.\ $0.79/0.73$ for the ungated control --- a $3$--$4\times$
reduction. RankMe stays $\sim$32--36 of 64, so the drop is genuine exclusion,
not collapse. That $z_{\mathrm{slow}}$ reads the regime \emph{better} than the
full embedding is itself the selective-use payoff: dropping fast dimensions
removes noise for the slow task.
\emph{Control --- unmixing does not suffice (Tab.~1):} one might object that
the ungated embedding already contains the factors and only needs a label-free
rotation. Applying SFA, ICA (slowest components), and PCA to the ungated
embedding and probing the extracted 16-dim subspace refutes this: each method
trades one side for the other --- SFA and ICA do cut fast leakage but lose the
regime (acc $0.57$/$0.52$), while PCA keeps the regime ($0.76$) and all the
leak ($0.80$). Only training-time gating keeps the slow factor ($0.80$)
\emph{and} excludes the fast one ($0.19$); the separation is created by the
objective, not recoverable post hoc.
\textbf{F2 --- latent targets are necessary (H3).} On raw-patch prediction
the identical gate leaves fast leakage at the ungated level ($u\,0.78$ vs.\
$0.81$; phase $0.96$ vs.\ $0.97$) --- essentially nothing, against
$0.79\!\to\!0.19$ for latent prediction. Completing the
latent/raw$\times$regression/contrastive grid, the same gate on a latent
\emph{contrastive} objective (CPC-InfoNCE against EMA embeddings, in-batch
negatives) separates as well or better: with dcor, regime $0.81$ with fast
leakage $0.05$ ($u$) / $0.00$ (phase), while $z_{\mathrm{fast}}$ retains both
fast factors ($0.85/0.95$ --- no collapse). So the mechanism is not
JEPA-regression-specific: horizon gating works whenever the target lives in
\emph{latent} space and fails when raw targets force fast detail into every
dimension. Consistent with F3, contrastive gate-alone also under-separates
($u$ $0.46$, phase $0.81$) --- dcor is again the purifier.
\textbf{F3 --- structure assigns, penalty purifies (H2).} Gate alone barely
lowers leakage ($u\,0.74$, phase $0.79$, near the ungated $0.79/0.73$); dcor
alone \emph{degenerates} --- the model satisfies decorrelation by emptying the
slow block (regime probe $0.55$). Only the combination separates ($0.19/0.27$):
the gate gives each block a job, the penalty removes duplicates. This is
consistent with H2, now formalized as Proposition~2
(Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}) --- the objective has no term that removes fast
information from $z_{\mathrm{slow}}$; exclusion is done by the bottleneck and
dcor, not by the gate alone. We also tested a variant with removal pressure added
(masking the slow dimensions of the \emph{target} at short horizons): it does
not rescue gate-alone, confirming the mechanism is bottleneck-based rather
than fixable by extra gating.
\textbf{F4 --- the mechanism is ``gate assigns, bottleneck excludes'', and it
scales.} Shrinking the slow block $16\!\to\!8$ dims cuts leakage to $0.08$
($u$) / $0.23$ (phase) at unchanged regime accuracy ($0.82$), while widening
to $32$ dims restores leakage ($0.78/0.87$). A width sweep makes the mechanism
precise: what matters is the \emph{absolute} slow-block size, not its fraction
of the embedding. Keeping a fixed 16-dim block while widening the backbone to
$d_z{=}128$ and $256$ preserves separation (fast leak $0.06$/$0.08$, regime
$\sim\!0.72$), whereas scaling the block proportionally ($32$/$64$ dims) lets
leakage flood back ($u\;R^2\!\approx\!0.6$). So the gate \emph{assigns} the
slow factor to the ungated block, and the narrow bottleneck (plus dcor)
\emph{excludes} the fast one --- and this transfers to wide backbones provided
the slow block stays narrow. The $\tau$ sweep is weaker
under the leak-free protocol (best near $2.5\,T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ but within
seed noise), which we note honestly. An earlier iteration \emph{failed}
informatively: when the fast factor's lifetime was shorter than the shortest
horizon, the gate had nothing to separate --- the effect requires genuine
multi-scale structure.
\emph{Nonlinear readout (reviewer-requested honesty check):} linear probes
overstate exclusion. An MLP probe recovers $u$ from $z_{\mathrm{slow}}$ at
$R^2\,0.45$ (phase $0.78$), vs.\ $0.19/0.27$ linear --- though still well
below the ungated control's $0.84/0.96$, and $z_{\mathrm{fast}}$ retains the
fast factors ($0.83/0.97$: displaced, not destroyed). A MINE estimate agrees:
$I(z_{\mathrm{slow}};u)\!\approx\!0.95$ nats with the gate vs.\ $1.21$
without (and $1.21$--$1.23$ in $z_{\mathrm{fast}}$). Our claims should
therefore be read as \emph{linear-subspace} separation plus a partial
information-level reduction --- not information-theoretic removal.

\textbf{F5 --- real data, group-split (Fig.~3, Tab.~2).} With held-out
subjects/patients (leak-free, and a domain-shift test), the gate reproduces
the synthetic separation ($n{=}3$ seeds). HAPT (slow = activity persisting
$\sim$12\,s, fast = gait, $T_{\mathrm{ac}}\!\sim\!0.1$\,s): gate cuts fast
leakage $0.56\!\to\!0.17$ while keeping activity F1 $0.71$ (vs.\ full $0.80$)
--- and this holds on \emph{unseen subjects}, evidence for the selective
domain-robustness the method is meant to give. PTB-XL ECG: a smaller but
consistent effect (fast leakage $0.61\!\to\!0.41$ with dcor). On the AR head
the gate does nothing on both (H3). RankMe ($\sim$26--36) confirms no collapse.
The decorrelation penalty \emph{hurts} on HAPT yet \emph{helps} on PTB-XL ---
its value is data-dependent, and the weaker PTB-XL effect is expected since
diagnostic cues partly live at the beat timescale (a static-content setting,
which we flag as a limitation). On XJTU-SY bearing vibration we
obtained three results instead of a separation showcase.
(i) A naive low-pass baseline cannot recover the fault-carrying envelope
($R^2{=}{-}0.19$ vs.\ $0.65$ for the learned embedding). We initially read
this as motivation evidence, but flag a correction after adding the
\emph{proper} classical tool: our stored envelope proxy is itself computed by
the Hilbert transform, so probing Hilbert-envelope features against it is
circular ($R^2\!\approx\!1$ by construction) --- the envelope-proxy comparison
is retracted as evidence.
(ii) The non-circular test is the health trajectory (lifetime fraction) on
\emph{held-out bearings}: there Hilbert-envelope features fail to transfer
($R^2{=}{-}1.08$; low-pass $-0.11$), while the learned embedding is weakly
positive, and specifically its slow block ($z_{\mathrm{slow}}$ $0.18$ $\ge$
full $0.18$ $>$ $z_{\mathrm{fast}}$ $0.09$, 3 seeds) --- health information,
to the modest extent it is captured at all, sits where the gate puts slow
factors. We state plainly that cross-bearing RUL on XJTU is weak for
\emph{every} method tested.
(iii) Measured autocorrelation shows the accessible carrier and envelope
proxies share nearly the same timescale ($T_{\mathrm{ac}}\!\approx\!1$--$2$
patches), and accordingly the gate does \emph{not} manufacture separation ---
consistent with F4: the method separates only where a genuine within-window
timescale gap exists.
\textbf{F6 --- a theory-bearing competitor; standard metrics.} An
architecture-matched SlowVAE (Klindt et al., 2021: reconstruction + Laplace
transition prior, \emph{with} identifiability theory), same backbone, data and
protocol: its 16 slowest latents show moderate leakage ($0.27/0.29$) but lose
the slow factor (regime $0.63$; even its \emph{full} embedding decodes only
$0.54$) --- reconstruction is local, so a factor requiring integration over
hundreds of steps never enters the code. On reviewer-requested metrics, MIG is
near zero for every method (it rewards one-dimensional codes; ours is a block
code), and DCI-disentanglement ranks SlowVAE ($0.66$) \emph{above} ours
($0.39$) even though SlowVAE cannot decode the regime --- the known failure of
axis-aligned metrics that ignore informativeness (Locatello et al., 2019). We
therefore keep the block probe matrix, with absolute scores, as the operative
measure and report DCI/MIG for comparability.

\section{Anomaly attribution: a negative pilot}
The motivating payoff was \textbf{anomaly attribution}: a spike in the
\emph{short}-horizon residual signals a point anomaly; a spike in the
\emph{long}-horizon residual (slow-block dims, median over anchors for
target-contamination robustness) signals contextual drift. We tested this
with typed injected anomalies (3-step spikes; 400-step frequency shifts with
no regime switch, both on- and off-manifold). Result: point detection works
(short-residual AUROC $0.93$--$0.97$), but \textbf{contextual detection is at
chance} ($\approx\!0.50$) for gated and ungated models alike, in both
injection designs. Diagnosis: the encoder, never having seen the shifted
dynamics, \emph{projects them onto normal slow codes} --- there is no novelty
response in $z_{\mathrm{slow}}$ for the residual to catch. Attribution
therefore currently works only in the direction the reviewer conceded was
easy (point), and the contextual direction needs novelty-sensitive machinery
(e.g.\ training-time exposure to drift, or density models on
$z_{\mathrm{slow}}$), not just the gate. We report this as a genuine negative
of the present design rather than defer it.

\section{Planned work and limitations}
Next: contextual-anomaly machinery as diagnosed above, then C-MAPSS
(avoiding the label-quality issues of SMAP/PSM). Also: (i) further external baselines under matched
budgets (TS2Vec, masked reconstruction); an amplitude-modulation
scenario where filter decomposition fails by construction; (ii) an
envelope-analysis (Hilbert) baseline on XJTU in place of naive low-pass;
(iii) replacing EMA with SIGReg
(HEPA/LeNEPA) and showing the gate effect is stabilizer-independent.
\textbf{Limitations and assumptions.} Exclusion is soft (H2) and impossible to
make complete when fast dynamics are conditioned on slow state; the dcor
penalty helps on some data, hurts on others. The two-block, single-$\tau$
design assumes \emph{exactly two} timescales --- $k$ scales would need $k$
blocks and a gate schedule, untested. Setting $\tau=c\,T_{\mathrm{ac}}$
assumes the fast dynamics dominate the signal's autocorrelation; if the slow
factor also drives large-amplitude structure, $T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ overestimates
the fast lifetime. Both timescales are measured in \emph{patches}, so patch
length and sampling rate implicitly bound the separable range. Suitability
requires a genuine timescale gap in the window, which is diagnosable
pre-training (XJTU was correctly non-separable); factors that complete their
dynamics within one patch are invisible to the gate. Validation still needs
factor labels --- standard for disentanglement; training is fully label-free.
