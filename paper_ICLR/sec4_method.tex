\section{Method}

\subsection{Model}
\label{sec:method_model}

\textbf{Encoder.} A patch becomes a token through one linear projection, so channels are mixed at the first layer --- HAPT posture is defined by how gravity distributes over the three axes. Tokens pass through causal transformer layers with rotary position encoding, and a final linear layer produces the 64-dimensional embedding, of which $\zslow$ is the leading 16 coordinates and $\zmix$ the remaining 48. A per-block LayerNorm is applied last, to the two blocks separately rather than to the embedding as a whole (Section~\ref{sec:xcov}).

\textbf{Predictor.} A small MLP reading the embedding at one time point together with the horizon, which enters as a learned expansion of $\log_2\Delta$. One predictor is conditioned on $\Delta$ rather than one head per horizon.

The attention operation is unmodified: the gate sits between encoder output and predictor input, the penalty in the loss, the per-block LayerNorm in the encoder's last line. This is a change of training procedure, not of architecture, so it attaches to any causal sequence encoder.

\textbf{Target.}
\begin{equation}
z^{*} = \mathrm{sg}\big[\,f_{\bar\theta}\big(x_{[t+\Delta-w,\;t+\Delta]}\big)_{-1}\big], \qquad \bar\theta \leftarrow m\bar\theta + (1-m)\theta
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}

The prediction target is not the raw signal but the representation an EMA copy of the encoder reads from a short window inside the future interval, with the gradient stopped. Bounding that window keeps the target's receptive field inside $(t,\,t+\Delta]$, so it never re-contains the anchor's own past and the shortcut of matching the target from what is already known at the anchor is closed by construction. \textbf{The target never passes the gate and is always the full embedding}: at far horizons the model is asked to match the whole future embedding from the persistent block alone, and this asymmetry is the core of the mechanism.

\textbf{Loss.}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{xcov}}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} \in \{\mathcal{L}_{1},\ \mathcal{L}_{\text{NCE}}\}
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}

The two forms share backbone, gate and penalty and differ only in the loss layer. \textbf{HGLP-Reg} uses the $L_1$ distance; under $L_2$ the squared-error gradient is dominated by large residuals and results swung across seeds. \textbf{HGLP-NCE} uses InfoNCE over cosine similarity with temperature 0.1, with one departure from the standard form: \textbf{samples from the same window are excluded from the negative pairs}, since they share the persistent factor and pushing them apart would teach the model to discard it.


\subsection{The Horizon Gate}
\label{sec:gate}

\begin{equation}
g(\Delta) = \sigma\!\Big(\frac{\tau-\Delta}{\kappa}\Big), \qquad \tilde z_t(\Delta) = \big[\,z_t^{\mathrm{per}}\,;\ g(\Delta)\,z_t^{\mathrm{mix}}\,\big]
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}

Immediately before the predictor, the rear block is multiplied by $g(\Delta)\in(0,1)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}(b)--(d)): for $\Delta\ll\tau$ the embedding passes unchanged, for $\Delta\gg\tau$ the rear block is suppressed. \emph{Dimensions are multiplied, not truncated}, so the predictor input is always $D$-dimensional; \emph{$\zslow$ is never blocked}; and the gate is \emph{a smooth curve, not a step}. The trained horizons must straddle the threshold, $\Delta_{\min}<\tau<\Delta_{\max}$, or the gate never opens or never closes. The width $\kappa$ is a buffer rather than a convenience, since $\tau$ cannot be chosen accurately without labels, and a step gate was worse on both stems (Appendix~\ref{app:design}).

The one-sidedness is likewise a choice. Withholding $\zmix$ beyond $\tau$ should cost nothing, because the transient factor is already forgotten there. Withholding $\zslow$ below $\tau$ would cost a great deal: near horizons need the current persistent state as much as the fast detail, and they are where the gradients are dense, so the block would lose most of its training signal rather than gain protection. Appendix~\ref{app:design} tests the symmetric variant. Note also that only the \emph{availability} of $\zmix$ is constrained, never its content --- across the six settings it carries the persistent factor at \numMixPerLo--\numMixPerHi{} --- which is why we call it $\zmix$ and not $z_{\mathrm{tra}}$.

\textbf{Gradient split.}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial z_t^{\mathrm{per}}} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \tilde z_t^{\mathrm{per}}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial z_t^{\mathrm{mix}}} = g(\Delta)\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \tilde z_t^{\mathrm{mix}}}
\label{eq:grad}
\end{equation}

Because the encoder is shared across horizons, this one line splits the training signal the two blocks receive: $\zslow$ is updated at every horizon, whereas $\zmix$ is updated in proportion to $g(\Delta)$ and so effectively only at near horizons. Two consequences follow, and only one of them is guaranteed. \textbf{Availability is enforced}: whatever still contributes to prediction beyond $\tau$ must be readable in $\zslow$, since no predictor however large can restore information the gate has suppressed to zero. \textbf{Exclusion is not enforced}: $\zslow$ is still used at near horizons, where transient information lowers the loss, and no term of the objective pushes that information \emph{out of} $\zslow$ --- exclusion has to come from the penalty of Section~\ref{sec:xcov}, and we measure it rather than assume it.

\subsection{Pulling the Two Blocks Apart}
\label{sec:xcov}

\textbf{Cross-covariance penalty.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
C &= \tfrac{1}{N-1}\,\bar Z^{\top}_{(1:\dslow)}\,\bar Z_{(\dslow+1:D)},\\[2pt]
\mathcal{L}_{\text{xcov}} &= \tfrac{1}{\dslow (D-\dslow)}\textstyle\sum_{a,b} C_{ab}^{2}
\end{split}
\label{eq:xcov}
\end{equation}

The penalty pushes the two blocks to be linearly unpredictable from each other and \textbf{acts on the embedding before the gate}. It travels with the \textbf{per-block LayerNorm}: a single LayerNorm over the whole embedding gives the two blocks one shared mean and variance, recreating exactly the coupling the penalty is trying to remove. \textbf{``Mechanism-free'' therefore means all three devices absent} --- gate, penalty and per-block LayerNorm.
