\section{Motivation and claim}
Practitioners want the \emph{slowly-varying state} of a time series (machine
health, diagnosis, activity) \emph{separated} from fast variation, not merely
co-encoded: separation is what enables selective domain robustness and
interpretable state trajectories. Frequency decomposition
\cite{mallat1999wavelet,huang1998empirical} fails in principle --- a slow
factor need not live in a low band (bearing health rides the envelope of
high-frequency resonance \cite{randall2011rolling}); sequential VAEs
(DSAE \cite{hsu2017unsupervised}, C-DSVAE \cite{bai2021contrastively})
separate only \emph{static} content via fragile reconstruction; and
horizon-conditioned JEPAs (HEPA \cite{hepa2026}) encode multi-scale dynamics
without factoring them.

Our observation: \textbf{the prediction horizon --- free in any predictive
learner --- is itself a supervision axis}, because only slow information
helps predict the far future
\cite{bialek2001predictability,creutzig2009past}. \emph{H1: gating a latent
block out of the predictor at long horizons concentrates slow factors in the
ungated block, with no labels.} Two refinements set the claim's exact
strength. \emph{H2 (assignment asymmetry):} the gate \emph{assigns} slow
information (provable); \emph{exclusion} of fast information rests on a
narrow bottleneck plus a decorrelation penalty. \emph{H3:} the gate requires
\emph{latent} targets; raw-signal prediction smears detail everywhere. Unlike
slow feature analysis \cite{wiskott2002slow} we select by predictive value,
not trajectory slowness; unlike temporal nonlinear-ICA
(TCL \cite{hyvarinen2016unsupervised}, SlowVAE \cite{klindt2021towards}) we
have no full identifiability theorem --- our inductive bias, in the sense
required by \cite{locatello2019challenging}, is the timescale gap itself.

\section{Method}
A causal transformer encoder ($\sim$0.5M params) maps patches to
$z_t=[z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t;z^{\mathrm{fast}}_t]\in\mathbb R^{64}$ (16 dims
slow). A predictor conditioned on $\Delta\in\{1,4,16,64,128\}$ patches
regresses the EMA target encoder's embedding (CPC multi-horizon predictor
\cite{oord2018representation} with I-JEPA-style targets
\cite{assran2023self,baevski2022data2vec}, as in HEPA \cite{hepa2026}; our
contribution is the gate, not the backbone):
\[
\mathcal L=\textstyle\sum_{\Delta}\big\|
P\big(z^{\mathrm{slow}}_t, g(\Delta)z^{\mathrm{fast}}_t, \Delta\big)
-\bar z_{t+\Delta}\big\|^2+\lambda\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dcor}},
\]
with soft gate $g(\Delta)=\sigma((\tau-\Delta)/w)$, where $\sigma$ is the
logistic sigmoid and $w$ (4 patches) sets the transition width, so the fast
block fades smoothly from open ($g{\approx}1$) to closed ($g{\approx}0$)
around $\tau$. \textbf{Why asymmetric.} The design mirrors an asymmetry in the
generative process. (i) Fast dynamics are \emph{conditioned on} the slow
state --- the next gait sample needs the current activity, the next
oscillation sample needs the regime's carrier --- so short-horizon targets
need both blocks. (ii) The fast factor forgets itself within
$T_{\mathrm{ac}}$, so the far future needs only the slow block. A gate should
therefore remove only the information that has become useless: closing the
fast path at long horizons is harmless. The symmetric variant, closing the
slow path at short horizons, would instead cut information the short-horizon
loss still needs, forcing the encoder to copy slow information into
$z^{\mathrm{fast}}$ --- the very duplication separation must prevent.
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{dcor}}$ penalizes squared cross-covariance
(VICReg-lineage \cite{bardes2022vicreg}). $\tau=c\,T_{\mathrm{ac}}$
($c{\approx}2.5$) comes from the signal's autocorrelation decay --- label-free,
and doubling as a pre-training applicability screen.

\section{Theory (proofs in extended report)}
\textbf{Prop.~1 (inclusion).} If the far future is conditionally independent
of the past given the slow state ($\Delta\ge\Delta_0$: the timescale gap) and
the gate is closed at some trained $\Delta^*\!\ge\!\Delta_0$, every population
minimizer makes $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$ mean-sufficient for the far future; with
affinely independent regime-conditional target means, the slow-state
posterior is a function of $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$. \emph{(Square-loss tower
decomposition.)}
\textbf{Prop.~2 (no exclusion).} In plain terms: the objective itself cannot
force fast information out of $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$, so exclusion has to come
from the architecture. Formally, for any minimizer a measure-preserving
re-encoding can hide any fast statistic inside $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$ at
identical loss, so exclusion is \emph{not identified} by the objective under
any gate schedule, and dcor removes only \emph{linearly}-readable
duplication. The nonlinear leak we measure is therefore this boundary, not a
bug. What excludes in practice is the encoder's smoothness bias: a
bounded-Lipschitz network does not realize the pathological re-encoding, only
approximates it, so exclusion is empirical (cf.\ F4). A uniqueness theorem,
and Prop.~1's condition that regimes differ in the \emph{mean} not only in
variance, remain open.

\section{Experiments}
\textbf{Datasets (Tab.~1).} Each dataset supplies a labeled \emph{slow}
factor (used only to \emph{verify} separation) and a measurable \emph{fast}
factor (to quantify leak); training never sees labels.

\emph{Synthetic two-timescale oscillator} --- the mechanism testbed, with
recorded ground truth for both factors. The observation is a single
oscillating signal $x_t=\sin\varphi_t+0.2\,u_t+\text{noise}$. The
\textbf{slow factor} is a hidden 3-state regime $s_t$ (Markov chain, mean
dwell 3000 steps) whose \emph{only} effect is setting the carrier frequency
($\pm5\%$). The \textbf{fast factor} is an OU process $u_t$ (memory
$\approx$50 steps) that jitters the instantaneous frequency by a comparable
amount, plus the oscillation phase itself. Amplitudes are identical across
regimes and the regime's frequency shift is within the jitter range, so
\emph{no short-window statistic reveals the regime} --- identifying it
requires integrating hundreds of steps, exactly the long-horizon information
the gate is meant to isolate.

\emph{HAPT} \cite{reyes2016transition} --- smartphone IMU: the quantity of
interest (activity, persisting $\sim$12\,s) is buried under $\sim$1\,Hz gait
oscillation (\textbf{slow} $=$ activity, \textbf{fast} $=$ acceleration
magnitude).
\emph{PTB-XL} \cite{wagner2020ptb} --- 12-lead ECG (\textbf{slow} $=$
normal-vs-abnormal ``NORM'' diagnosis, binary macro-F1; \textbf{fast} $=$
beat waveform). An acknowledged \emph{limiting} case: diagnosis is constant
over a 10\,s record --- the \emph{static}-content regime we fault DSAE for ---
so we treat it as a stress test, not a showcase.
\emph{XJTU-SY} \cite{wang2020xjtu} --- bearing vibration, included as the
\emph{applicability boundary}: intended slow $=$ health, fast $=$ carrier,
but both live at one timescale within a window, so the method predicts
non-separation (F5).

\textbf{Protocol.} Probes are fit on training groups and evaluated on held-out
groups (Tab.~1, ``held out'') over disjoint windows, so no near-duplicate
window crosses the split and HAPT/PTB-XL double as domain-shift tests. We
report \emph{absolute} scores (accuracy / $R^2$ / macro-F1), with RankMe
\cite{garrido2023rankme} monitoring collapse so a low leak cannot be an
artifact of an empty block. $n{=}3$ seeds, one backbone and budget for all
variants; one patch is 8 steps, so horizons $\{1{,}4{,}16{,}64{,}128\}$ span
$8$--$1024$ steps. Results are findings F1--F5.

\textbf{F1 --- separation, and alternatives do not suffice (Fig.~2A, Tab.~2).}
Gate+dcor keeps the regime in $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$ while cutting fast leak
roughly fourfold. It even reads the regime slightly better than the full
embedding: dropping fast dimensions removes noise for the slow task. Neither
shortcut substitutes. Label-free rotations of the ungated embedding
(SFA/ICA/PCA) each trade one side for the other (Tab.~2), so separation is
created by the objective, not recoverable post hoc. A theory-bearing
competitor, an architecture-matched SlowVAE \cite{klindt2021towards}, recovers
the regime only weakly --- its reconstruction is local, so a factor needing
integration over hundreds of steps barely enters its code.

\textbf{F2 --- latent targets, either objective (Fig.~2A).} The identical gate
on raw-patch (AR) targets separates nothing: raw targets force fast detail
into every dimension. On a latent \emph{contrastive} objective (CPC-InfoNCE)
it separates as well or better, with $z^{\mathrm{fast}}$ still holding the
fast factors (no collapse). So the mechanism needs a latent target, but is
not specific to JEPA regression.
\textbf{F3 --- gate assigns, penalty purifies (Fig.~2A; Prop.~2).} Gate-alone
barely reduces leak. dcor-alone degenerates, satisfying decorrelation by
emptying the slow block. Only the combination separates: the gate gives each
block a job, the penalty removes duplicates. Adding target-side removal
pressure does not rescue gate-alone, matching Prop.~2.
\textbf{F4 --- exclusion rides on capacity, and scales (Fig.~2C).} Leak tracks
the \emph{absolute} slow-block size. A fixed 16-dim block keeps its exclusion
when the backbone widens to $d_z{=}256$, whereas a proportionally scaled block
lets leak flood back. So the mechanism transfers to wide backbones, provided
the slow block stays narrow.
\textbf{F5 --- real data (Fig.~3, Tab.~3).} On HAPT the gate cuts fast leak
sharply while keeping activity F1, and this holds on \emph{unseen subjects}
--- the selective robustness the method is meant to give. On PTB-XL the effect
is smaller and needs dcor; whether dcor helps or hurts is data-dependent
(its \emph{polarity} flips between datasets) and not yet decidable without
labels. XJTU is the predicted negative: carrier and health share one timescale
within a window, so the gate correctly does nothing. There the non-circular
health target (lifetime fraction, held-out bearings) is hard for every method,
and the learned $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$ carries what little exists. The classical
envelope and low-pass features do worse, but that is a repurposing failure,
not a verdict on classical practice: envelope analysis is built for
fault-frequency \emph{detection}, not cross-bearing regression. We read XJTU
only as a correctly-predicted non-separable case.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\textbf{Limitations.} Exclusion is soft and \emph{linear-level}: nonlinear
probes recover more of the fast factor than linear ones (MLP $R^2$ $0.45$
vs.\ $0.19$), which is exactly the boundary Prop.~2 identifies --- our claim
is linear-subspace separation, not information-theoretic removal. Most importantly, on real data the demonstrated benefit is so far the
\emph{internal} separation metric (lower leak); the payoffs the introduction
motivates (robustness under a fast-dynamics shift, interpretable
trajectories, attribution) are not yet measured there, and gated training in
fact has a downstream cost (HAPT: full embedding $0.80\!\to\!0.76$;
$z^{\mathrm{slow}}$ alone $0.71$). The synthetic win of $z^{\mathrm{slow}}$
over the full embedding ($0.80$ vs.\ $0.74$) is in-distribution selective
use, not robustness-under-shift; an explicit shift experiment is the first
priority. The dcor polarity is data-dependent and not yet
decidable label-free; $\tau$ is label-free but
$d_{\mathrm{slow}},\lambda$ are not. The design assumes exactly two
timescales, and $\tau=c\,T_{\mathrm{ac}}$ assumes fast dynamics dominate the
ACF. Training is fully label-free; validation, as standard in
disentanglement, is not.
\textbf{Future work (in progress or planned).} A shift-robustness experiment
(above); unsupervised model selection (a health-metric criterion currently
rejects broken configs but cannot fine-tune); anomaly attribution via
horizon-split residuals (point detection works in a pilot, contextual does
not); more datasets with pre-registered gap screening (Sleep-EDF
\cite{kemp2000analysis}, C-MAPSS \cite{saxena2008damage}); external baselines
(TS2Vec \cite{yue2022ts2vec}, masked reconstruction); and a nonlinear (HSIC)
penalty for the linear-level limitation.
